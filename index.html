Long-term view

What changed, and why it works

Each of these answers something specific in the master plan. The full write-up is in docs/UX_DESIGN_TRACKING_PLAN.md.

The big one · replaces §53

Ask the question, then answer it

The plan's headline output was “Gold Score: 72/100.” Nobody wants that. The screen now prints the question a person actually has — Should I buy gold today? — and answers it in four words: Yes — but only part.

The score still runs the engine and is still stored for accountability. It just stops being the headline.

New idea

The price slider answers “then when?”

Every buy/wait app has the same hole: it tells you to wait, and you have no idea what you're waiting for. Drag the slider and the answer, gauge and money recompute live — so you can see that at ₹1,44,750 this becomes a clear buy.

It makes the model inspectable without a single technical word.

Comprehension

A gauge everyone can already read

A speedometer needs no legend. Five zones, a needle, and the verdict in words — readable in about a second by someone who has never invested.

Colour is never the only channel: every state also carries a word, a position on the arc and a shape, so it survives colour-blindness and greyscale.

Experience · promotes §23

The ladder does the hard part

Staged buying was buried at §23. As the spine of the app it removes the impossible task — you're never asked to time the market, only to tick a box when told.

It also absorbs a wrong call: if today's verdict is off, only 30% of the money is committed.

Design · fixes §52

Gold as identity, colour as meaning

The plan used orange for both “press this” and “worry about this”. Now gold is the brand, and a green→amber→red ramp owns status — the ordering people already expect, so there's nothing to learn.

Tracking · fixes §36

The price you actually pay

Costs appeared only inside backtesting — yet the same ₹50,000 buys about 3.25g as an ETF and about 2.84g as jewellery. A 12% making charge dwarfs any timing edge the model could earn.
